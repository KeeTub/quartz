# Acts 16

<span class="v-num">1</span> And he came to Derbe and Lystra; and behold, a certain disciple was there, by name Timothy, <span class="added-text">(the)</span> son of a certain believing Jewish woman, but <span class="added-text">(the)</span> father a Greek,

<span class="v-num">2</span> who was well spoken of by the brothers in Lystra and Iconium.

<span class="v-num">3</span> This one Paul wanted to go forth with him, and having taken <span class="added-text">(him)</span> he circumcised him, because of the Jews who were in those places; for all knew his father, that he was a Greek.[^1]

<span class="v-num">4</span> And as they went through the cities, they delivered to them to keep the decrees having been decided by the apostles and the elders who were in Jerusalem.

<span class="v-num">5</span> So then the churches were being strengthened in the faith, and were increasing in number daily.

<span class="v-num">6</span> And having gone through Phrygia and the Galatian region, having been forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word in Asia,[^2]

<span class="v-num">7</span> having come to Mysia, they tried to go into Bithynia; and the Spirit did not allow them.

<span class="v-num">8</span> And having passed by Mysia, they came down to Troas.

<span class="v-num">9</span> And a vision appeared to Paul through the night: a certain man, a Macedonian, was standing, urging him and saying, "Having crossed over into Macedonia, help us."

<span class="v-num">10</span> And when he saw the vision, immediately we sought to go out into Macedonia, concluding that the Lord had called us to proclaim the gospel to them.

<span class="v-num">11</span> Having set sail therefore from Troas, we ran a straight course to Samothrace, and the following <span class="added-text">(day)</span> to Neapolis,

<span class="v-num">12</span> and from there to Philippi, which is <span class="added-text">(the)</span> first city of the district of Macedonia, a colony. And we were staying in this city some days,

<span class="v-num">13</span> and on the day of the Sabbaths we went outside the city beside a river, where it was supposed a place of <span class="alt-label">prayer</span><span class="alt-text">(=prayer meeting)</span> to be; and having sat down, we spoke to the women who had gathered together.

<span class="v-num">14</span> And a certain woman by name Lydia, a seller of purple of the city of Thyatira, one who worshiped God, was listening, whose heart the Lord opened, to attend to the things spoken by Paul.[^3]

<span class="v-num">15</span> And when she was baptized, and her household, she urged <span class="added-text">(us)</span>, saying, "If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, having entered into my house, stay," and she constrained us.

<span class="v-num">16</span> And it happened, as we were going to <span class="added-text">(the place of)</span> prayer, a certain slave girl having a spirit of Python met us, who provided much <span class="alt-label">profit</span><span class="alt-text">(=gain)</span> to her masters by fortune-telling.

<span class="v-num">17</span> This one, having followed after Paul and us, kept crying out, saying, "These men are servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to us a way of salvation."

<span class="v-num">18</span> And this she did for many days; but Paul, being greatly troubled, and having turned, said to the spirit, "I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her." And it came out that very hour.

<span class="v-num">19</span> And her masters, having seen that the hope of their profit was gone, having seized Paul and Silas, dragged <span class="added-text">(them)</span> into the marketplace to the rulers,

<span class="v-num">20</span> and having brought them to the magistrates, said, "These men, being Jews, are throwing our city into confusion,

<span class="v-num">21</span> and are proclaiming customs which are not lawful for us to receive nor to do, being Romans."

<span class="v-num">22</span> And the crowd rose up together against them, and the magistrates, having torn off their garments, commanded <span class="added-text">(them)</span> to be beaten with rods;

<span class="v-num">23</span> and having laid many blows on them, they threw <span class="added-text">(them)</span> into prison, commanding the jailer to keep them safely,

<span class="v-num">24</span> who, having received such a command, threw them into the inner prison, and fastened their feet securely in the stocks.

<span class="v-num">25</span> And about midnight Paul and Silas, praying, were singing hymns to God; and the prisoners were listening to them.

<span class="v-num">26</span> And suddenly a great earthquake occurred, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken; and immediately all the doors were opened, and everyone's bonds were loosed.

<span class="v-num">27</span> And the jailer, having become awake, and having seen the doors of the prison opened, having drawn a sword, was about to kill himself, supposing the prisoners to have escaped.

<span class="v-num">28</span> But Paul cried with a loud voice, saying, "Do nothing harmful to yourself, for we are all here."

<span class="v-num">29</span> And having asked for lights, he rushed in, and having become trembling, fell down before Paul and Silas;

<span class="v-num">30</span> and having brought them out, he said, "Sirs, what must I do that I may be saved?"[^4]

<span class="v-num">31</span> And they said, "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household."[^5]

<span class="v-num">32</span> And they spoke to him the word of the Lord, and to all who were in his house.

<span class="v-num">33</span> And having taken them in that hour of the night, he washed <span class="added-text">(them)</span> from their wounds, and he was baptized, he and all his, immediately.

<span class="v-num">34</span> And having brought them up into his house, he set <span class="added-text">(before them)</span> a table, and rejoiced greatly, with his whole household, having believed in God.

<span class="v-num">35</span> And when day came, the magistrates sent the rod-bearers, saying, "Release those men."

<span class="v-num">36</span> And the jailer reported these words to Paul, "The magistrates have sent that you be released; now therefore, having gone out, go in peace."[^6]

<span class="v-num">37</span> But Paul said to them, "Having beaten us publicly, uncondemned, men who are Romans, they threw <span class="added-text">(us)</span> into prison; and now do they cast us out secretly? No indeed; but having come themselves, let them bring us out."

<span class="v-num">38</span> And the rod-bearers reported these words to the magistrates; and they were afraid, having heard that they were Romans,

<span class="v-num">39</span> and having come, they urged them, and having brought <span class="added-text">(them)</span> out, they asked <span class="added-text">(them)</span> to go out of the city.

<span class="v-num">40</span> And having gone out of the prison, they entered into <span class="added-text">(the house of)</span> Lydia; and having seen the brothers, they encouraged them, and departed.

[^1]: 바울이 이방인에게는 할례를 강요하지 않았으나(갈2장) 유대인 어머니를 둔 디모데의 사역 편의를 위해 유대 관습을 따른 것으로, 복음의 자유와 지혜로운 순응이 함께 나타난다.
[^2]: 성령께서 인격적으로 선교의 길을 막고 여신 것으로, 하나님의 주권적 인도가 인간의 계획보다 앞섬을 보여준다.
[^3]: διήνοιξε(열어주셨다)는 부정과거로, 루디아의 믿음이 인간의 결단이 아니라 주님의 주도적 은혜로 시작되었음을 강조한다.
[^4]: ἵνα+가정법(σωθῶ)은 '구원받기 위해 무엇을 해야 하는가'라는 목적을 나타내는 목적절이다. 아직 이루어지지 않은 일에 대한 절박한 질문의 뉘앙스를 담고 있다.
[^5]: πίστευσον(믿어라)은 부정과거 명령으로 단회적 결단을 요구하며, 개인의 믿음이 온 집안 구원의 통로가 됨을 보여준다.
[^6]: ἵνα+가정법(ἀπολυθῆτε)은 관리들이 사람을 보낸 목적('놓아주기 위해')을 나타내는 목적절이다. 즉 석방 명령의 의도를 직접 밝히는 구문이다.
