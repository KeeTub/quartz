<span class="v-num">1</span> And when it was decided that we should sail to Italy, they delivered Paul and certain other prisoners to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan cohort.[^1]

<span class="v-num">2</span> And having boarded a ship of Adramyttium, being about to sail to the places along Asia, we set sail, Aristarchus a Macedonian of Thessalonica being with us.

<span class="v-num">3</span> And the next <span class="added-text">(day)</span> we landed at Sidon; and Julius, treating Paul kindly, permitted <span class="added-text">(him)</span>, having gone to <span class="added-text">(his)</span> friends, to receive care.

<span class="v-num">4</span> And from there having set sail, we sailed under <span class="added-text">(the lee of)</span> Cyprus, because the winds were contrary.

<span class="v-num">5</span> And having sailed across the sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came down to Myra of Lycia.

<span class="v-num">6</span> And there the centurion, having found an Alexandrian ship sailing to Italy, put us in it.

<span class="v-num">7</span> And sailing slowly for many days, and having come with difficulty off Cnidus, the wind not permitting us <span class="added-text">(further)</span>, we sailed under <span class="added-text">(the lee of)</span> Crete, off Salmone.

<span class="v-num">8</span> And sailing along it with difficulty, we came to a certain place called Fair Havens, near which was the city of Lasea.

<span class="v-num">9</span> And much time having passed, and the voyage being now dangerous, because the fast also was already past, Paul advised <span class="added-text">(them)</span>,[^2]

<span class="v-num">10</span> "Men, I see that the voyage is about to be with injury and much damage, not only of the cargo and the ship, but also of our lives."

<span class="v-num">11</span> But the centurion trusted the pilot and the shipowner more than the things spoken by Paul.

<span class="v-num">12</span> And the harbor being unsuitable for wintering, the majority set <span class="added-text">(their)</span> plan to set sail from there also, if perhaps they might be able, having arrived at Phoenix, to winter <span class="added-text">(there)</span>, a harbor of Crete facing <span class="added-text">(the)</span> southwest and <span class="added-text">(the)</span> northwest.

<span class="v-num">13</span> And a south <span class="added-text">(wind)</span> blowing softly, thinking they had obtained their purpose, having weighed anchor, they sailed close along Crete.

<span class="v-num">14</span> But not long after, a tempestuous wind, called Euroclydon, beat down from <span class="alt-label">it</span><span class="alt-text">(=against it)</span>.[^3]

<span class="v-num">15</span> And the ship being caught <span class="added-text">(by the storm)</span>, and not being able to face the wind, having given <span class="added-text">(it)</span> up, we were driven along.

<span class="v-num">16</span> And running under a certain small island called Clauda, we were with difficulty able to gain control of the boat,

<span class="v-num">17</span> which having hoisted up, they used helps, undergirding the ship; and fearing lest they should fall into the Syrtis, having lowered the gear, so they were driven along.

<span class="v-num">18</span> And we being exceedingly tossed by the storm, the next <span class="added-text">(day)</span> they began to throw <span class="added-text">(the cargo)</span> overboard;

<span class="v-num">19</span> and on the third <span class="added-text">(day)</span> we cast out with our own hands the ship's tackle.

<span class="v-num">20</span> And neither sun nor stars appearing for many days, and no small storm pressing <span class="added-text">(upon us)</span>, from then on all hope of our being saved was being taken away.

<span class="v-num">21</span> "Men, you ought to have listened to me and not to have set sail from Crete, and to have gained this injury and damage."

<span class="v-num">22</span> "And now I urge you to be of good cheer; for there will be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship."

<span class="v-num">23</span> "For this night an angel of the God, whose I am, and whom I serve, stood by me,"

<span class="v-num">24</span> "saying, 'Do not be afraid, Paul; you must stand before Caesar; and behold, God has granted you all those sailing with you.'"

<span class="v-num">25</span> "Therefore be of good cheer, men; for I believe God, that it will be just as it was told to me."

<span class="v-num">26</span> "But we must run aground on a certain island."

<span class="v-num">27</span> And when the fourteenth night had come, as we were driven about in the Adriatic <span class="added-text">(Sea)</span>, about midnight the sailors surmised that some land was drawing near to them;

<span class="v-num">28</span> and having sounded, they found twenty fathoms; and having gone a little farther, and having sounded again, they found fifteen fathoms.

<span class="v-num">29</span> And fearing lest we should fall upon rocky places, having cast four anchors out of the stern, they prayed for day to come.

<span class="v-num">30</span> And the sailors seeking to flee out of the ship, and having let down the boat into the sea, under pretense as though they were about to lay out anchors from the bow,

<span class="v-num">31</span> Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, "Unless these remain in the ship, you cannot be saved."[^4]

<span class="v-num">32</span> Then the soldiers cut off the ropes of the boat, and let it fall away.

<span class="v-num">33</span> And until it was about to become day, Paul urged them all to partake of food, saying, "Today is the fourteenth day you have been waiting and continuing without food, having taken nothing."

<span class="v-num">34</span> "Therefore I urge you to take food; for this is for your <span class="alt-label">safety</span><span class="alt-text">(=preservation)</span>; for not a hair from the head of any of you will perish."

<span class="v-num">35</span> And having said these things, and having taken bread, he gave thanks to God before all, and having broken <span class="added-text">(it)</span>, he began to eat.

<span class="v-num">36</span> And all being encouraged, they also themselves took food.

<span class="v-num">37</span> And we were in the ship, all the souls, two hundred <span class="added-text">(and)</span> seventy-six.[^5]

<span class="v-num">38</span> And having eaten enough food, they lightened the ship, throwing the wheat into the sea.

<span class="v-num">39</span> And when day came, they did not recognize the land; but they observed a certain bay having a beach, into which they resolved, if they could, to run the ship ashore.

<span class="v-num">40</span> And having cast off the anchors, they left <span class="added-text">(them)</span> in the sea, at the same time loosening the ropes of the rudders; and having hoisted up the foresail to the blowing <span class="added-text">(wind)</span>, they made for the shore.

<span class="v-num">41</span> But having fallen into a place where two seas met, they ran the ship aground; and the bow, having stuck fast, remained unmoved, but the stern was being broken by the force of the waves.

<span class="v-num">42</span> And the soldiers' plan was that they should kill the prisoners, lest any, having swum out, should escape.[^6]

<span class="v-num">43</span> But the centurion, wanting to save Paul, prevented them from <span class="added-text">(their)</span> plan, and commanded those who could swim, having thrown <span class="added-text">(themselves)</span> first <span class="added-text">(into the sea)</span>, to go out to the land,

<span class="v-num">44</span> and the rest, some on planks, and some on certain things from the ship. And so it came about that all were brought safely to land.

[^1]: σπείρης Σεβαστῆς(아구스도 부대)는 로마 보조군단 명칭으로, 바울이 로마 황제 직속 계통 부대의 백부장에게 압송되었음을 보여준다.
[^2]: νηστεία(금식)는 유대력 속죄일(대속죄일, 9~10월경) 금식을 가리키며, 이는 지중해 항해가 위험해지는 늦가을 시기임을 암시한다.
[^3]: Εὐροκλύδων(유라굴로)은 동풍(εὖρος)과 파도(κλύδων)의 합성어로 추정되는 지중해 특유의 강풍 명칭이다.
[^4]: ἐάν+가정법(μείνωσιν)은 아직 실현되지 않은 미래의 가능성을 전제하는 3류 조건문으로, '만일 ~하지 않으면'이라는 실제적 경고의 뉘앙스를 담는다. 선원들의 잔류가 구원의 필수 조건임을 강조한다.
[^5]: TR은 276명(διακόσιαι ἑβδομηκονταέξ)으로 읽으나 일부 사본(예: 바티칸 사본)은 76명으로 읽는 이문이 있다.
[^6]: ἵνα+가정법(ἀποκτείνωσι)이 목적절을 이루어 '죽이려는' 의도를 나타낸다.
