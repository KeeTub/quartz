<span class="v-num">1</span> Festus therefore, having taken office <span class="added-text">(in)</span> the province, after three days went up to Jerusalem from Caesarea.

<span class="v-num">2</span> And the high priest and the chief <span class="added-text">(men)</span> of the Jews made <span class="added-text">(charges)</span> known to him against Paul, and were entreating him,

<span class="v-num">3</span> asking <span class="added-text">(a)</span> favor against him, that he might send for him to Jerusalem, making an ambush to kill him along the way.

<span class="v-num">4</span> Festus then answered that Paul was being kept in Caesarea, and <span class="added-text">(that)</span> he himself was about to depart shortly.

<span class="v-num">5</span> "Let therefore the powerful <span class="added-text">(men)</span> among you," he says, "having gone down with <span class="added-text">(me)</span>, if there is anything in this man, accuse him."

<span class="v-num">6</span> And having stayed among them more than ten days, having gone down to Caesarea, the next day, having sat on the judgment seat, he commanded Paul to be brought.

<span class="v-num">7</span> And when he had arrived, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around, bringing many and heavy charges against Paul, which they were not able to prove,

<span class="v-num">8</span> he defending himself, "For neither against the law of the Jews, nor against the temple, nor against Caesar have I sinned in anything."

<span class="v-num">9</span> But Festus, wanting to lay down a favor with the Jews, answering Paul, said, "Do you wish, having gone up to Jerusalem, to be judged there concerning these things before me?"

<span class="v-num">10</span> But Paul said, "I am standing at the judgment seat of Caesar, where I ought to be judged. I have done the Jews no wrong, as you also know very well."

<span class="v-num">11</span> "For if indeed I do wrong and have done anything worthy of death, I do not refuse to die; but if there is nothing <span class="added-text">(in)</span> those things of which these <span class="added-text">(men)</span> accuse me, no one can give me up to them. I appeal to Caesar."[^1]

<span class="v-num">12</span> Then Festus, having conferred with the council, answered, "You have appealed to Caesar; to Caesar you shall go."

<span class="v-num">13</span> And after some days had passed, Agrippa the king and Bernice arrived at Caesarea, to greet Festus.

<span class="v-num">14</span> And as they were staying there many days, Festus laid before the king the <span class="added-text">(matters)</span> concerning Paul, saying, "There is a certain man left behind by Felix <span class="added-text">(as)</span> a prisoner,

<span class="v-num">15</span> concerning whom, when I was at Jerusalem, the chief priests and the elders of the Jews made <span class="added-text">(charges)</span> known, asking judgment against him;

<span class="v-num">16</span> to whom I answered, that it is not <span class="added-text">(the)</span> custom of <span class="added-text">(the)</span> Romans to give up any man to destruction, before the <span class="added-text">(one)</span> accused has the accusers face to face, and receives opportunity of defense concerning the charge.

<span class="v-num">17</span> Therefore, when they had come together here, having made no delay, the next <span class="added-text">(day)</span>, having sat on the judgment seat, I commanded the man to be brought;

<span class="v-num">18</span> concerning whom, the accusers, having stood up, brought no charge of such things as I was supposing,

<span class="v-num">19</span> but had certain questions against him concerning their own religion, and concerning a certain Jesus <span class="added-text">(who was)</span> dead, whom Paul asserted to be alive.

<span class="v-num">20</span> And I, being at a loss concerning the inquiry about this, was saying, if he wished to go to Jerusalem, and there be judged concerning these things."

<span class="v-num">21</span> "But Paul, having appealed to be kept for the decision of the <span class="alt-label">Emperor</span><span class="alt-text">(=Augustus)</span>, I commanded him to be kept, until I might send him to Caesar."

<span class="v-num">22</span> And Agrippa said to Festus, "I myself was also wishing to hear the man." "Tomorrow," he says, "you shall hear him."

<span class="v-num">23</span> Therefore, on the next <span class="added-text">(day)</span>, when Agrippa and Bernice had come with much pomp, and had entered into the audience hall, together with the chiliarchs and the men of eminence of the city, and <span class="added-text">(when)</span> Festus had commanded, Paul was brought.

<span class="v-num">24</span> And Festus says, "King Agrippa, and all the men present with us, you see this <span class="added-text">(man)</span>, concerning whom all the multitude of the Jews petitioned me, both in Jerusalem and here, crying out that he ought not to live any longer."

<span class="v-num">25</span> "But I, having perceived <span class="added-text">(that)</span> he had done nothing worthy of death, and <span class="added-text">(since)</span> he himself also had appealed to the <span class="alt-label">Emperor</span><span class="alt-text">(=Augustus)</span>, I decided to send him."

<span class="v-num">26</span> "Concerning whom I have nothing certain to write to <span class="added-text">(my)</span> lord; therefore I brought him before you, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that, the examination having taken place, I might have something to write."

<span class="v-num">27</span> "For it seems to me unreasonable, sending a prisoner, not also to signify the charges against him."
