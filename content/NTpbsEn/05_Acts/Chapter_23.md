# Acts 23

<span class="v-num">1</span> Paul, having looked intently at the council, said, "Men, brothers, I have lived in all good conscience before God until this day."[^1]

<span class="v-num">2</span> And <span class="added-text">(the)</span> high priest Ananias commanded those standing by him to strike his mouth.

<span class="v-num">3</span> Then Paul said to him, "God is about to strike you, whitewashed wall! And you sit judging me according to the law, and command me to be struck, transgressing the law?"

<span class="v-num">4</span> But those standing by said, "Do you revile the high priest of God?"

<span class="v-num">5</span> And Paul said, "I did not know, brothers, that he is <span class="added-text">(the)</span> high priest; for it is written, 'You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people.'"

<span class="v-num">6</span> But Paul, knowing that the one part were Sadducees and the other Pharisees, cried out in the council, "Men, brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of a Pharisee; concerning <span class="added-text">(the)</span> hope and resurrection of <span class="added-text">(the)</span> dead I am being judged."

<span class="v-num">7</span> And when he had said this, a dissension arose between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the multitude was divided.

<span class="v-num">8</span> For <span class="added-text">(the)</span> Sadducees say there is no resurrection, neither angel nor spirit, but <span class="added-text">(the)</span> Pharisees confess both.[^2]

<span class="v-num">9</span> And there arose a great outcry; and the scribes of the Pharisees' part arose and contended fiercely, saying, "We find nothing evil in this man; but if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him, let us not fight against God."

<span class="v-num">10</span> And when a great dissension had arisen, the <span class="alt-label">chiliarch</span><span class="alt-text">(=commander)</span>, fearing lest Paul should be torn in pieces by them, commanded the <span class="alt-label">soldiers</span><span class="alt-text">(=army)</span> to go down and snatch him out of their midst, and to bring <span class="added-text">(him)</span> into the barracks.

<span class="v-num">11</span> And on the following night, the Lord stood by him and said, "Be of good courage, Paul; for as you have testified the things concerning me at Jerusalem, so you must testify also at Rome."

<span class="v-num">12</span> And when day came, some of the Jews formed a <span class="alt-label">conspiracy</span><span class="alt-text">(=made a plotting together)</span>, binding themselves under a curse, saying <span class="added-text">(that they would)</span> neither eat nor drink until they had killed Paul.[^3]

<span class="v-num">13</span> And there were more than forty who had made this conspiracy.

<span class="v-num">14</span> who, having come to the chief priests and the elders, said, "We have bound ourselves under a curse to taste nothing until we have killed Paul.

<span class="v-num">15</span> Now therefore, you, with the council, notify the <span class="alt-label">chiliarch</span><span class="alt-text">(=commander)</span>, that he may bring him down to you tomorrow, as though you were about to inquire more precisely the things concerning him; but we, before he comes near, are ready to kill him."

<span class="v-num">16</span> But the son of Paul's sister, having heard of the ambush, came and entered into the barracks and reported <span class="added-text">(it)</span> to Paul.

<span class="v-num">17</span> And Paul, having called one of the centurions to him, said, "Take this young man to the <span class="alt-label">chiliarch</span><span class="alt-text">(=commander)</span>; for he has something to report to him."

<span class="v-num">18</span> So he, having taken him, brought <span class="added-text">(him)</span> to the <span class="alt-label">chiliarch</span><span class="alt-text">(=commander)</span>, and said, "Paul the prisoner called me to him and asked <span class="added-text">(me)</span> to bring this young man to you, who has something to say to you."

<span class="v-num">19</span> And the <span class="alt-label">chiliarch</span><span class="alt-text">(=commander)</span>, having taken hold of his hand, and having withdrawn privately, inquired, "What is it that you have to report to me?"

<span class="v-num">20</span> And he said, "The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow, as though they were about to inquire something more precisely concerning him.

<span class="v-num">21</span> You therefore, do not be persuaded by them; for more than forty men of them lie in ambush for him, who have bound themselves under a curse neither to eat nor drink until they have killed him; and now they are ready, waiting for the promise from you."

<span class="v-num">22</span> So the <span class="alt-label">chiliarch</span><span class="alt-text">(=commander)</span> let the young man go, commanding <span class="added-text">(him)</span>, "Tell no one that you have revealed these things to me."

<span class="v-num">23</span> And having called two certain of the centurions, he said, "Prepare two hundred soldiers, that they may go as far as Caesarea, and seventy horsemen, and two hundred spearmen, from <span class="added-text">(the)</span> third hour of the night;

<span class="v-num">24</span> and <span class="added-text">(to)</span> provide beasts, that having set Paul <span class="added-text">(on one)</span>, they may bring <span class="added-text">(him)</span> safely to Felix the governor,"[^4]

<span class="v-num">25</span> having written a letter containing this <span class="alt-label">form</span><span class="alt-text">(=content)</span>:

<span class="v-num">26</span> "Claudius Lysias to the most excellent governor Felix, <span class="alt-label">greetings</span><span class="alt-text">(=Rejoice)</span>.

<span class="v-num">27</span> This man was seized by the Jews, and was about to be killed by them, when I came upon <span class="added-text">(them)</span> with the <span class="alt-label">soldiers</span><span class="alt-text">(=army)</span> and rescued him, having learned that he is a Roman.

<span class="v-num">28</span> And wanting to know the reason for which they accused him, I brought him down to their council;

<span class="v-num">29</span> whom I found to be accused concerning questions of their law, but having no charge worthy of death or of bonds.

<span class="v-num">30</span> And it having been reported to me that there would be a plot against the man by the Jews, I sent <span class="added-text">(him)</span> to you immediately, having commanded the accusers also to state before you the things against him. Farewell."

<span class="v-num">31</span> So the soldiers, according to what was ordered them, took Paul and brought <span class="added-text">(him)</span> through the night to Antipatris.

<span class="v-num">32</span> And on the next day, having let the horsemen go with him, they returned to the barracks;

<span class="v-num">33</span> who, having entered into Caesarea, and having delivered the letter to the governor, presented Paul also to him.

<span class="v-num">34</span> And the governor, having read <span class="added-text">(it)</span>, and having asked from what province he was, and having learned that <span class="added-text">(he was)</span> from Cilicia,

<span class="v-num">35</span> "I will hear you fully," he said, "whenever your accusers also arrive." And he commanded him to be kept in Herod's <span class="alt-label">praetorium</span><span class="alt-text">(=palace)</span>.

[^1]: πεπολίτευμαι는 완료시제로, 바울이 지금까지 일관되게 선한 양심으로 살아온 상태를 강조한다.
[^2]: 부활·천사·영에 대한 견해 차이는 당대 두 유대 분파의 신학적 대립을 요약적으로 보여준다.
[^3]: ἀνεθεμάτισαν(저주로 맹세하다)은 자신을 엄중한 저주 아래 두는 서약으로, 그들의 결의의 절박함을 드러낸다.
[^4]: ἵνα+가정법(διασώσωσι)은 목적절로, 짐승을 준비시키는 행동의 목적(바울을 안전히 호송하기 위함)을 나타낸다.
