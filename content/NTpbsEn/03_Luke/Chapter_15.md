<span class="v-num">1</span> Now all the tax collectors and the sinners were coming near to him, to hear him.

<span class="v-num">2</span> And the Pharisees and the scribes were murmuring, saying, "This man receives sinners and eats with them."

<span class="v-num">3</span> And he spoke this parable to them, saying,

<span class="v-num">4</span> "What man of you, having a hundred sheep and having lost one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness and go after the one having been lost, until he finds it?

<span class="v-num">5</span> And having found <span class="added-text">(it)</span>, he lays <span class="added-text">(it)</span> on his own shoulders, rejoicing,

<span class="v-num">6</span> and having come to the house, he calls together <span class="added-text">(his)</span> friends and <span class="added-text">(his)</span> neighbors, saying to them, 'Rejoice with me, for I found my sheep, the one having been lost.'

<span class="v-num">7</span> I say to you that thus there will be joy in heaven over one sinner repenting, more than over ninety-nine righteous <span class="added-text">(ones)</span> who have no need of repentance.

<span class="v-num">8</span> Or what woman having ten drachmas, if she loses one drachma, does not light a lamp and sweep the house and seek carefully until she finds <span class="added-text">(it)</span>?[^1]

<span class="v-num">9</span> And having found <span class="added-text">(it)</span>, she calls together <span class="added-text">(her)</span> women friends and <span class="added-text">(her)</span> neighbors, saying, 'Rejoice with me, for I found the drachma which I lost.'

<span class="v-num">10</span> Thus, I say to you, there is joy before the angels of God over one sinner repenting."

<span class="v-num">11</span> "A certain man had two sons;

<span class="v-num">12</span> and the younger of them said to <span class="added-text">(his)</span> father, 'Father, give me the portion of the property falling to <span class="added-text">(me)</span>.' And he divided to them the <span class="alt-label">living</span><span class="alt-text">(=property)</span>.

<span class="v-num">13</span> And not many days after, the younger son, having gathered all together, went away into a far country, and there he squandered his property, living <span class="alt-label">recklessly</span><span class="alt-text">(=in dissipation)</span>.

<span class="v-num">14</span> And when he had spent all, there arose a mighty famine throughout that country, and he began to be in want.

<span class="v-num">15</span> And having gone, he joined himself to one of the citizens of that country, and he sent him into his fields to feed swine.

<span class="v-num">16</span> And he was longing to fill his belly with the pods which the swine were eating, and no one was giving to him.

<span class="v-num">17</span> But having come to himself, he said, 'How many hired servants of my father have abundance of bread, but I am perishing here with hunger![^2]

<span class="v-num">18</span> I will rise and go to my father, and I will say to him, Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you,

<span class="v-num">19</span> and I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me as one of your hired servants.'

<span class="v-num">20</span> And having risen, he went to his own father. But while he was yet far off, his father saw him and was moved with compassion, and having run, he fell on his neck and kissed him.[^3]

<span class="v-num">21</span> And the son said to him, 'Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you, and I am no longer worthy to be called your son.'

<span class="v-num">22</span> But the father said to his servants, 'Bring out the <span class="alt-label">first</span><span class="alt-text">(=best)</span> robe and put <span class="added-text">(it)</span> on him, and give a ring for his hand, and sandals for <span class="added-text">(his)</span> feet;

<span class="v-num">23</span> and having brought the fattened calf, kill <span class="added-text">(it)</span>, and having eaten, let us be merry;

<span class="v-num">24</span> for this my son was dead, and is alive again; and he was lost, and is found.' And they began to be merry.[^4]

<span class="v-num">25</span> Now his elder son was in the field; and as he came and drew near to the house, he heard music and dancing;

<span class="v-num">26</span> and having called one of the servants, he inquired what these things might be.

<span class="v-num">27</span> And he said to him, 'Your brother has come, and your father has killed the fattened calf, because he received him back in <span class="alt-label">health</span><span class="alt-text">(=safe and sound)</span>.'

<span class="v-num">28</span> But he was angry and did not want to go in. Therefore his father, having come out, was entreating him.

<span class="v-num">29</span> But he, answering, said to his father, 'Behold, so many years I have been serving you, and I never transgressed a commandment of yours, and you never gave me a young goat, that I might be merry with my friends;[^5]

<span class="v-num">30</span> but when this your son, who has devoured your living with harlots, came, you killed for him the fattened calf.'

<span class="v-num">31</span> And he said to him, 'Child, you are always with me, and all <span class="added-text">(that is)</span> mine is yours.

<span class="v-num">32</span> But it was fitting to be merry and to rejoice, for this your brother was dead, and is alive again; and he was lost, and is found.'"[^6]

[^1]: ἐάν+가정법(ἀπολέσῃ)은 아직 일어나지 않았지만 충분히 일어날 수 있는 미래 가능성을 나타내는 조건절이다. '만일 잃어버린다면'이라는 실제적 가정으로, 이후 여인의 행동(등불 켜고 찾음)이 그 조건에 대한 자연스러운 반응임을 보여준다.
[^2]: εἰς ἑαυτὸν ἐλθὼν은 '제정신이 들다, 자기 자신을 돌아보다'라는 관용구로 탕자의 회개가 시작되는 결정적 전환점을 나타낸다.
[^3]: ἐσπλαγχνίσθη(불쌍히 여기다)는 창자(내장)에서 우러나는 깊은 긍휼을 뜻하는 단어로, 아버지의 조건 없는 사랑의 깊이를 강조한다.
[^4]: '죽었다가 살아났으며 잃었다가 찾았다'는 구절이 24절과 32절에 동일하게 반복되어 비유 전체를 감싸는 인클루지오를 이루며 회개의 핵심 주제를 강조한다.
[^5]: ἵνα+가정법(εὐφρανθῶ)은 '내게 염소를 주었다면 그 목적은 친구들과 즐기기 위함이었을 텐데'라는 목적절로, 아버지가 주지 않은 것에 대한 큰아들의 서운함과 억울함을 강조한다.
[^6]: 24절과 동일한 어구가 반복되어 비유의 결론을 이루는 인클루지오로서, 죽음에서 생명으로, 잃음에서 찾음으로의 전환이 회개의 본질임을 재확인시킨다.
